Privacy policy

What CineTrack knows about you. It isn't much.

Last updated 21 November 2026

CineTrack ("the app") helps you track the films of awards season, fill in a ballot and play in pools with friends. It is made by Afonso Salcedo, an individual developer. This page says what the app does with your information, in plain words.

The short version

  • There is no sign-up. The app never asks for your email address, phone number or real name.
  • Your films, ratings, watch dates and ballot stay on your devices and in your own iCloud.
  • If you join a pool, the display name you choose, your ballot progress, your score and, once the ceremony starts, your picks are stored online so the other members of that pool can see them.
  • The app counts anonymous usage, such as how many people have it open during the ceremony. This is not linked to your name or email address.
  • There is no advertising, and you are not tracked across other apps or websites.
  • Nothing is sold or shared for marketing.

What stays on your device and in your iCloud

The films you mark as watched, your star ratings, the dates you watched them, films you add yourself, your Up Next queue, your ballot picks and your nomination predictions are saved on your device. If you are signed in to iCloud, they also sync between your own devices through Apple's iCloud key-value storage. That copy lives in your iCloud account. The developer cannot read it. Apple's handling of iCloud data is covered by Apple's privacy policy.

What is stored online when you use pools

Pools are optional. They run on Google's Firebase Realtime Database, which the developer manages. To tell members apart without accounts, the app signs in to Firebase anonymously the first time it runs. This creates a random identifier for your installation. It is not linked to your name, email address or Apple Account.

When you create or join a pool, this is stored:

WhatWho can see it
The pool's name and its join codeMembers of the pool, and anyone who has the code
The display name you choose (up to 40 characters)Members of the pool
When you joined, how much of your ballot is filled in, and your scoreMembers of the pool
Your ballot picks, saved once when the ceremony startsMembers of the pool

A pool can be read only by its members. Your picks are not uploaded before the ceremony starts, so nobody in the pool can copy them. Choose a display name you are happy for the other members to see; it does not need to be your real name.

The app also reads the list of winners from the same database during the ceremony. That is a download only.

Anonymous usage statistics

CineTrack includes Google Analytics for Firebase. It tells the developer how the app is being used in general: how many people have it open, roughly how long for, which version they are on, and whether things keep working when everyone opens the app at once on ceremony night.

It collects:

  • a random identifier for your installation of the app, which is reset if you delete the app;
  • basic usage events, such as opening the app and moving between its screens;
  • your device model, iOS version, app version, language and approximate region, such as your country, worked out from your IP address. The IP address itself is not stored.

It does not collect your name, email address, the films you watch, your ratings or your ballot. It is not used for advertising, the app does not use Apple's advertising identifier, and this information is not combined with data from other apps or websites. Google processes it on the developer's behalf under Google's privacy policy. Usage data is kept for up to 14 months.

Game Center

If you are signed in to Game Center on your device, CineTrack sends your progress and scores to Apple's Game Center leaderboards and achievements, where they appear under your Game Center name according to your Game Center settings. The app may suggest your Game Center nickname as a pool display name; you can change it. Game Center is run by Apple under Apple's privacy policy, and you can sign out of it in the Settings app.

Other services the app talks to

These requests carry no account and no identifier for you. As with any internet request, the service sees your IP address.

  • TMDB (The Movie Database), for film details, posters, cast, trailers and streaming availability. The app sends the films and people it is looking up, and the text you type when you search for a film to add.
  • GitHub, to download each season's list of shortlisted and nominated films.
  • Letterboxd, only if you connect it. The app reads the public diary feed for the username you enter and marks those films as watched. Nothing is sent to Letterboxd, and the app never asks for your Letterboxd password. Your username is stored only on your device. Disconnecting removes it.
  • YouTube, the App Store and streaming apps, only when you tap a trailer or a Where to Watch link. Those open outside CineTrack under their own policies.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Calendar, only if you tap Add to Calendar on a nominations or ceremony reminder. The app adds that one event and does not read your other events.
  • Notifications, for reminders before the ceremony. They are scheduled on your device. The app has no server that sends you notifications.

The app does not ask for your location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone.

What the developer receives

  • Pool data and anonymous usage statistics, as described above.
  • Beta requests. If you ask for a TestFlight invite by email, the email address you give is used to send the invite and to write to you about the beta. It is not shared or used for marketing, and it is deleted when the beta ends, or sooner if you ask.
  • Email. If you write to the support address, your email address and whatever you include are used to reply to you and for nothing else.
  • TestFlight. If you use a beta build, Apple shares with the developer your tester email if you were invited by email, the feedback and screenshots you choose to send, and crash reports.
  • App Store. Apple gives developers aggregate, non-identifying figures such as download counts, and crash data if you have opted in to sharing it with developers.

How long pool data is kept, and how to delete it

  • Leaving a pool before the ceremony removes your display name, progress and membership from it.
  • Deleting a pool, which its host can do, removes the pool for every member.
  • After a ceremony, the pool and its final results are kept so members can look back at them. Pools from past seasons are read-only.
  • Deleting the app removes its data from that device. To remove the iCloud copy as well, open Settings → your name → iCloud → Storage on your iPhone or iPad.
  • Anything else. To have your entries in a pool removed, including from a past season, email the address below with the pool's name or code and your display name. Because there are no accounts, that is how your entries are found. Requests are handled within 30 days.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to see, correct or delete the personal information held about you, and to object to how it is used. The only information of that kind CineTrack holds is the pool data described on this page. Write to the address below to use any of these rights.

This website

This website sets no cookies and runs no analytics. It remembers your light or dark choice in your browser. To show the countdown, the page downloads the season's dates from GitHub, which hosts both the site and that file and sees your IP address as any web host does.

Children

CineTrack is not directed at children under 13, and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date. If the app ever starts collecting something it does not collect today, that will be said plainly, here and in the app's release notes, before it happens.
